## Task
The CV already carries the details. The letter's job is the motivation: why the candidate wants this role at this company, and how their past experience backs that up. Describe experience in general terms (roles, domains, the kinds of problems they've worked on), not project-by-project detail. Write in the candidate's own voice: plain, direct, first person, like a capable person explaining themselves simply, not marketing copy.

Structure (keep this order):
1. Greeting: "Dear Hiring Team," unless the posting names a contact person.
2. Who I am and what I'm applying for (2-3 sentences): who the candidate is right now (from the CV, e.g. current degree and university, previous role and employer) and the exact role and company.
3. Why (1-2 paragraphs, the heart of the letter): the candidate's motivation for this role, taken from the motivation notes and the lane, connected to what this posting and company are about. Each reason is backed by the experience that makes it credible, described broadly: e.g. "in my last role I spent three years on pricing and experimentation, turning product questions into decisions", not a list of projects with metrics. At most one brief example, and no more than one number in the whole letter.
4. Practical close (1 short paragraph): availability (use the lane sentence above if it's not empty), then the practical facts for HR above, kept close to their wording (they answer what HR will ask: permit, hours, sponsorship), then a simple line that they'd be glad to talk.
5. Sign-off: "Kind regards," followed by the candidate's full name from the CV.

Style rules:
- 220-330 words in total. Short sentences; one idea per sentence. Everyday words over jargon.
- No em dashes or en dashes as punctuation: use a period, a comma or a colon.
- No flattery or hype: avoid "thrilled", "passionate", "excited to", "I'd welcome", "leverage", "at scale", "maps directly", "aligns with", "world-class", "cutting-edge".
- Mention the company by name; use what the posting says about the team or product only where it connects to a real reason in the motivation notes.
- Do not repeat the CV, do not mention grades, do not claim skills or motivations the sources don't show.
